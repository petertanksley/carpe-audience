# Carpe Audience: rules, process, and audit checklist

A working checklist for this skill, paraphrased from John-Michael Keyes, *Carpe Audience: Give Better
Presentations Despite PowerPoint* (The Keyes Firm, 2011). Page numbers are the book's printed pages.
This is a reference card, not a substitute for the book: the reasoning, the examples and the stories
behind every rule are his. Read it: it is a free download from the I Love U Guys Foundation,
<https://iloveuguys.org/downloads/Carpe-Audience.pdf>.

**[E]** marks a rule the book states; **[A]** marks this skill's adaptation for research talks in
Quarto RevealJS.

## Rules

### Text on slides
- **R1 [E]** Seven words or fewer on a slide (pp. 44, 63, 103).
- **R2 [E]** Quotes may run longer: stop talking, let the room read, then resume (p. 44). Charts are not exempt; he only says a graph "might" be (p. 44).
- **R3 [E]** Never show the words you are saying (p. 26; Mayer's redundancy effect).
- **R4 [E]** No bullets on screen; they belong in the notes (p. 63).
- **R5 [E]** Reject the "rule of six" (p. 44).
- **R6 [E]** No title on every slide; a heading appears only when it is the point (p. 45).
- **R7 [E]** Never close with "In summary"; close on the call to action or a callback (p. 45).
- **R8 [E]** No all-caps for emphasis; emphasis is the voice's job (p. 47).
- **R9 [E]** Spell-check every slide (p. 53).

### Typography
- **R10 [E]** One typeface, two or three sizes (p. 47).
- **R11 [E]** Sans-serif, heavy or condensed weight, tall x-height (pp. 47–48, 52).
- **R12 [E]** Tight leading: 0.8 in PowerPoint terms, about 0.95–1.0 as CSS line-height; tighten tracking (pp. 51–52, 55, 61).
- **R13 [E]** No AutoFit: size and position identical slide to slide, "registration" (pp. 47, 61, 91).
- **R14 [E]** Display-sized type, far above any 36-point minimum (p. 45).
- **R15 [E]** Type anchored top-left (pp. 60–61).
- **R16 [E]** Carry the fonts with the deck (p. 53). **[A]** Self-hosted fonts, `embed-resources: true`.

### Template, colour, background
- **R17 [E]** A template of 4–6 layouts plus a Power List layout (pp. 57, 59).
- **R18 [E]** Solid black background, white type; no gradients or decoration (pp. 57–58, 60).
- **R19 [E]** No colour on type (white, occasionally grey); colour comes from photographs (pp. 60, 90).
- **R20 [E]** A logo is optional: reversed, transparent, lower right, same spot on every layout but the blank one (p. 58).
- **R21 [E]** 16:9 (p. 59).

### Images
- **R22 [E]** No clip art (pp. 45, 69).
- **R23 [E]** One picture per slide, never a montage (p. 75).
- **R24 [E]** Full bleed, split screen, or a single element on black (pp. 29, 75).
- **R25 [E]** A small boxed photo only when resolution forces it (p. 75).
- **R26 [E]** At least the slide's pixel width, no jaggies (pp. 75, 90). **[A]** 1920 px today.
- **R27 [E]** Focal point on a rule-of-thirds line (p. 71).
- **R28 [E]** Never distort proportions (p. 77).
- **R29 [E]** Soft shadow or vignette under type on a photo, never the hard default shadow (pp. 76–77).
- **R30 [E]** No handshake stock photos (p. 66).
- **R31 [E]** Three minutes per image search, then change the metaphor (p. 69).
- **R32 [E]** Clear licences and credit images (pp. 69, 83, 120–123).

### Motion and transitions
- **R33 [E]** One transition everywhere, a 0.5–1 s fade (pp. 59, 61, 91).
- **R34 [E]** Animate only physical movement; never text, Power Lists or logos (p. 91). **[A]** No fragments on a Power List; it steps by duplicated slides.

### Timing and pacing
- **R35 [E]** About every 30 seconds, a change: slide, pause or voice (p. 35).
- **R36 [E]** A new story every 3–6 minutes (p. 35).
- **R37 [E]** 10-minute blocks, each with about three 2–5 minute stories, long then shorter, and a hook into the next block (pp. 29, 35, 41, 91).
- **R38 [E]** Rapid-fire runs: 3–5 slides in 6–8 seconds, then a pause (pp. 36, 45).
- **R39 [E]** A runtime at the foot of every slide's notes (p. 90).
- **R40 [E]** Plan the slot at rehearsed time plus 10% (p. 93).

### Structure and content
- **R41 [E]** Write the call to action before anything else (pp. 39–40, 90), and name the allies who support it (p. 39).
- **R42 [E]** Tag every story and slide: Call to Action, Background, Set the Stage, Break, or Transition. Untagged content is cut (pp. 39–41).
- **R43 [E]** The first slide is a hook, not a logo or a plain title (pp. 63, 77).
- **R44 [E]** Don't open with your biography (p. 36).
- **R45 [E]** Nothing depends on the internet (pp. 83, 91).

## Process

1. Reframe: you are giving a presentation, not doing a PowerPoint (p. 17).
2. Scout the audience, host and venue; keep versions per audience and slot (pp. 32, 98–99).
3. Campfire (linear) or sweat lodge (boardroom: interruptions, random access, two endings) (pp. 31–32, 103–104).
4. Call to action first, tiered for a mixed room, with allies (pp. 39–40).
5. Write the stories as prose, away from the computer (pp. 40–41).
6. Read each story aloud against a stopwatch and tag its type (p. 41).
7. Cadence worksheet: Time / Story / Type / Slide, in 10-minute blocks (pp. 41, 91).
8. Napkins: one per story, the few words or the picture; this is where seven words comes from (pp. 43–44).
9. Build the template (pp. 57–61).
10. Assemble ("slide herding"): story into the notes with a runtime, napkin words first, then images (pp. 89–91).
11. Rehearse standing, aloud, with the clicker, at least three passes; add 10% (p. 93).
12. Learn presenter view (p. 95).
13. On the day: test audio, video and clicker from the room; box breathing (pp. 98–101).
14. Afterwards: the handout and evaluations; review the recording (pp. 101, 107–112).

## Numbers and charts

- **Every number is a story:** the statistic, a human example, the statistic again (pp. 39, 67).
- **Would one number do?** Often a single big number beats a chart (pp. 33, 79).
- **A chart makes one point,** stated as one sentence in the notes; less data, more point; detail to the handout (p. 79).
- **Motion:** two or more frames on identical axes, where the data was, then where it is or is going; establish the source and its validity on frame one (p. 80).
- **Pie only when the parts make the whole,** built in sequence: topic, the point slice, the rest, the close (p. 80). Never stacked bars unless essential (pp. 79–80).
- **Chart type by purpose** (p. 81, after Few): ranking → bars; trend → line; part-to-whole → bars or a built pie; deviation → lines or bars; distribution → columns or a line; correlation → scatter with a trend line.
- **Colour:** Keyes picks it "for drama" (p. 80). **[A]** One accent on grey, on chart marks only.
- **On-screen units:** ordinals for rankings, bare percentages, big counts as words, contrast pairs ("Zero" / "Hundreds"), and a human-scale reframe ("One in a Million") (pp. 33, 36–37, 64).
- **Numbers serve every story type,** Transitions and Calls to Action included (p. 41).
- **Power List variants** (pp. 63–67): statistical series (one number per slide, fast, then slow on the punchline), common elements (a group of one-word items, one lit at a time), isolated info (one point and one image per slide), highlight the fact (topic, group unlit, each item lit with its statistic, group again).
- **Derived-number build** (pp. 36–37): a chain of counts and percentages ending on a human-scale reframe aimed at the call to action, under a minute.

## Audit checklist

1. A call to action is written down, and the close delivers it, not "In summary" or a bare "Questions?" (pp. 39–40, 45).
2. Every slide's notes carry a story-type tag and a runtime (pp. 40–41, 90).
3. No slide over seven words without a pause-and-read cue in the notes (p. 44).
4. No on-screen bullets; sequences are Power Lists (pp. 63–67).
5. Notes are not a copy of the slide text (pp. 21, 26).
6. The first slide is a hook; the speaker's biography is not the opening (pp. 36, 63).
7. Pacing: a change about every 30 s, a story every 3–6 min, a hook at each 10-minute mark, at least one rapid-fire run (pp. 35–36, 41, 45).
8. Planned runtime x 1.10 fits the slot (p. 93).
9. Typography: one sans-serif family, at most three sizes, heavy weight, tight leading, no all-caps emphasis, consistent registration (pp. 47–52, 91).
10. Background and colour: solid dark, white type, colour only in photos and data highlights (pp. 57–60, 90).
11. One image per slide, no montages; full bleed, split, or single element on black (pp. 29, 75).
12. Image quality: full slide width, no jaggies, proportions kept, focal point on the thirds, soft shadow under type (pp. 71, 75–77).
13. No clip art, handshakes or cliché stock; licences cleared and credited (pp. 45, 66, 69, 120).
14. Charts make one highlighted point; pies only for wholes; lines for trends; motion where change matters (pp. 79–81).
15. Detail lives in the handout, report or backup slides, not the main line (pp. 79, 103, 107).
16. One fade throughout; no animated text, logos or arrows (pp. 59, 61, 91).
17. Logo, if any: reversed, lower right, consistent, absent from the blank slide (p. 58).
18. Offline-safe: every font, image, video and sound bundled (pp. 53, 83, 91).
19. The handout is a separate document (notes with action items, questions, resources, contact, evaluation), given at the end; reports are prose (pp. 22, 103, 107–113).
20. Rehearsed at least three times aloud, standing, timed, with the recording reviewed (pp. 93, 95, 116).
